# Integrating Decision Trees and Natural Language Processing for Robust Click Fraud Detection: A Scalable and Adaptive Machine Learning Framework

Click fraud has been a persistent threat to the integrity and efficiency of pay-per-click advertising models, leading to inflated costs and misleading campaign analytics. Fraudulent activities, ranging from automated bot clicks to organized click farms, have resulted in inflated metrics and wasted advertising budgets. This study proposed a machine learning-based framework that leveraged Natural Language Processing (NLP) to classify clicks as legitimate, observational, or fraudulent. The proposed approach combined simplicity, interpretability, and computational efficiency, ensuring rapid decision-making while maintaining strong fraud detection capabilities. Using data from 10 traffic sources, the methodology incorporated feature engineering, text preprocessing, and robust model optimization techniques. Key textual features, such as HTTP headers and request metadata, were transformed into numerical representations using NLP, while feature selection and hyperparameter tuning ensured computational efficiency and accuracy. Stratified cross-validation confirmed the model’s effectiveness, achieving high balanced accuracy despite class imbalances. In addition to effective click classification, computational efficiency was evaluated due to the necessity of rapid response times to preserve user experience. The total processing time, including sample preprocessing and testing on the model, was analyzed, and the findings are reported in this study. This research highlights the importance of adaptable and interpretable machine learning techniques in mitigating fraudulent activities, fostering trust, and improving the reliability of digital advertising platforms.
